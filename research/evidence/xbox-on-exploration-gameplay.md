## Timestamped evidence

### 00:38–00:54 — game type and co-op
- Minecraft Dungeons II is described by the developer as an **action RPG** set in Minecraft.
- Familiar and new enemies are mentioned.
- Up to **four players** can play.
- Couch co-op and online play are both supported, and the developers describe mixing the two.

### 01:11–01:47 — inventory, armor and talismans
- The armor slot from the first game is split into four slots:
  - helmet
  - chestplate
  - leggings
  - boots
- A new item slot/category called **talismans** is described.
- Talismans grant passive bonuses that specialize a playstyle.
- Some talismans gain experience and become stronger as the player progresses.

### 01:47–02:21 — Tasty Bone / playstyle
- **Tasty Bone** is named as an example.
- It gives a **wolf companion** that fights alongside the player.
- The design philosophy is described as “you are what you wear,” rather than choosing a fixed class.

### 02:21–03:15 — Blacksmith / Echo Shards
- Blacksmith can bring lower item-power loot upward.
- Gear can spawn with random effects.
- **Echo Shards** are a special currency used to reroll gear effects.
- The speaker describes refining preferred/legendary-style loot rather than relying only on repeated drops.

### 03:15–04:20 — enchantment books / Inner Mines / jump attacks
- Enchantments are applied to gear.
- Instead of being built into gear as in the first game, players collect **enchantment books** in the world.
- Books can be applied repeatedly to suitable gear.
- **Inner Mines** is selected for the main-hand weapon.
- Its spoken effect: weapon attacks deploy **two mines** that explode in proximity.
- Jump attacks are explicitly described as new to the sequel.
- Firework Arrows are stated to return.

### 04:37–06:01 — interconnected world
- The map/world structure is contrasted with selecting isolated missions at a table.
- Developers call the sequel a **big interconnected world**.
- Outside the core quest line, players can enter procedurally generated dungeons.

### 07:07–07:45 — Mini Inventory / Guiding Line / revive
- **Mini Inventory** can be opened with **D-pad Up** in the demonstrated controller setup.
- It is designed so multiple co-op players can manage gear at the same time.
- A **guiding line** can be triggered if a player is lost.
- Multiple players reviving someone at once speeds up revival.

### 08:04–08:50 — Creeper Candle / Echo Shards
- **Creeper Candle** is named.
- It is placed, waits, then explodes and damages enemies around it.
- Randomly generated dungeon entrances can lead to Echo Shards.
- Echo Shards are described as useful for enchanting and upgrading merchants in town.

### 08:50–09:10 — Soul-Corrupted mobs
- **Soul-Corrupted mobs** are described as extra-tough enemies.
- Defeating them can produce Echo Shards.

### 10:33–11:44 — map / Copper Monstrosity
- The map contains indicators for side quests.
- **Copper Monstrosity** is explicitly named as a major story boss.
- The party is looking for **Supreme Evoker**, but instead encounters Copper Monstrosity.

### 12:00–12:40 — story Note Block
- After the fight, the characters pick up a **Note Block**.
- Developers say it is important to the story.
- A music-box symbolism question is acknowledged, but details are intentionally withheld.
